Empirical researchers routinely transform outcomes to report unitless effects and to address skewness or nonlinearity, yet many popular transformations make inference misleading because results change with the measurement units of the data. Using data from a randomized experiment, we demonstrate that natural rescaling can drastically alter conclusions under widely used transformations. We formalize a minimal coherence requirement for empirical analysis: re-expressing the same data in different units should not change $t$-statistics and not distort predicted values beyond mechanical rescaling. For transformed-outcome linear regressions, we establish an equivalence theorem that links these properties to logarithmic and power transformations. We distinguish among structural, conditional-mean, and conditional-median semi-elasticities, and emphasize that these targets require additional, often strong, assumptions, even for logarithmic specifications. When interest centers on conditional-mean semi-elasticities, we show that log and power are also the only link functions in generalized linear models and outcome transformations in Tobit specifications that deliver scale invariance.
