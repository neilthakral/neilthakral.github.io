We study how the timing of information about windfalls shapes spending decisions when consumers derive utility from anticipating future consumption through gain-loss experiences relative to anticipated outcomes. We show that longer waiting between news and access to funds lowers short-term spending and raises saving, even absent tight liquidity constraints. The framework also reproduces stylized facts about how consumption responds to the arrival of gains but not to news about gains, asymmetric smoothing of losses, and smaller MPCs for larger windfalls.
