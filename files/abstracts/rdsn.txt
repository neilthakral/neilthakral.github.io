This paper develops a model of reference-dependent preferences in which the descriptive social norm—the distribution of others' actions—enters utility as a stochastic reference point. The marginal benefit of norm adherence depends only on an individual's rank in the distribution, which yields testable predictions: rank feedback moves low performers most; small approval cues prevent high performers from reducing effort in response to feedback; more precise feedback can increase norm adherence; uncertainty can induce precautionary over-compliance; higher suggestions or examples raise actions; and lower types may not follow norm shifts among high types. We derive implications for the design of social-comparison information.
